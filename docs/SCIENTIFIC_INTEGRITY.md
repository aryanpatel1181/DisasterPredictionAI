# Scientific Integrity Guidelines - TerraGuard AI

## 1. Core Principles
TerraGuard AI operates under strict scientific standards regarding natural hazards, risk modeling, and public emergency alerting.

### 1.1 Earthquake Forecasting Rules
- **No Deterministic Prediction**: TerraGuard AI strictly NEVER claims to deterministically predict an earthquake's exact future time, location, and magnitude.
- **Valid Capabilities**: Earthquake features are restricted to:
  1. Live seismic monitoring (USGS, EMSC, GDACS).
  2. Historical seismic frequency analysis (Gutenberg-Richter b-value, Omori law aftershock decay).
  3. Ground-shaking estimates (Peak Ground Acceleration / MMI estimates).
  4. Population exposure & building vulnerability overlay.
  5. Tsunami trigger potential (based on depth, location, magnitude, and focal mechanism).
  6. Official EEW (Earthquake Early Warning) alerts where published by recognized national geological surveys.

### 1.2 Data Type Distinction
To prevent confusion between raw data, forecasts, AI estimates, and official government warnings, TerraGuard AI enforces strict semantic and visual separation across all components:

| Category | Definition | Visual Indicator | Source Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| `OBSERVED` | Confirmed physical measurement from sensors/satellites | Cyan / Solid border | USGS GeoJSON, NASA GPM IMERG |
| `FORECAST` | Output from numerical weather/hydrodynamic models | Purple / Dashed border | ECMWF IFS, GloFAS River Discharge |
| `AI_RISK_ESTIMATE` | Calibrated probabilistic output generated by TerraGuard ML models | Amber-to-Red Dynamic Badge | XGBoost/LightGBM Risk Engine |
| `OFFICIAL_WARNING` | Formally issued emergency alert from national meteorological/emergency agencies | Glowing Red Alert Box with Authority Seal | WMO CAP, NOAA, GDACS Orange/Red Alert |

### 1.3 Disclaimer & Decision Support Policy
TerraGuard AI is explicitly designed as an experimental decision-support tool. It must never supersede official warnings issued by national emergency management authorities (e.g., USGS, NOAA, GDACS, WMO, JMA, MeteoFrance).
